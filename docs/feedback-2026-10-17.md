# Website feedback review - 17th October 2026

Feedback on [thefriendsofashley.org](https://www.thefriendsofashley.org) forwarded by Nick from parents and committee contacts in October 2026, with an assessment of each item and Nick's decisions in his own words.

This is a temporary working document. Delete it once every item is actioned or moved into [`ACTIONS.md`](ACTIONS.md) and [`PROJECT-BRIEF.md`](PROJECT-BRIEF.md).

**General sentiment from the reviewers:** "pretty brilliant", "nicely structured and informative", with nothing missing compared with other PTA sites.

## 1. Promote the site with a QR code

**Feedback received:** Is it worth having a QR code for the site and just getting it out there so people use it? It is a great resource, but we need to make sure everyone knows it is there.

**Assessment:** Cheap, quick and probably the highest-value item. The site only helps if parents know it exists, and nothing on the site itself needs to change: the QR is for posters, newsletters and the school gate.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Do it. "Create me a QR code - but it won't go on the website." Done: QR codes produced for the homepage (<https://www.thefriendsofashley.org/>) and the Newsletter page (<https://www.thefriendsofashley.org/newsletter/>), delivered via WhatsApp for print and sharing.

## 2. A year view of events

**Feedback received:** Liked another school's layout giving a year view and then the blurb of each event: date, time, requirement and contact.

**Assessment:** Worth doing and the clearest win of the bigger items. What's On today is a single chronological list, and a month or term grid is easier to scan. The real cost is keeping dates and contacts current, not building the page.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Likes it. "I like that page with the months and dates laid out like that. It's much clearer than the list we see on What's On. Prototype a page - show me some options." In progress: three design options prototyped (month grid, year strip with detail cards, term by term) on the prototype branch, with mobile and desktop screenshots shared for review.

## 3. A feedback section

**Feedback received:** Is it worth having a feedback section, for what people think is done well and so on?

**Assessment:** Useful only if someone reads and acts on the replies, so keep it light: a visible, inviting route to a short form. A feedback form link already exists in the autumn newsletter and the Contact page carries a form, but nothing on the site itself invites feedback.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Wanted. "I think this is needed - we could use Form Spree to do this (I use that for Sam's website). I just don't know how to make this interesting / engaging. I want to make sure people actually click on it and give feedback! Again, prototype some designs." In progress: three form designs prototyped (quick reaction, two questions, pinned note) with a placeholder Formspree action. To go live, Nick creates a form in his Formspree account pointed at the shared inbox and supplies the form ID.

## 4. Live pre-loved uniform stock list

**Feedback received:** Liked seeing pre-loved uniform availability on another school's site, with categories and product counts.

**Assessment:** The Uniform page already covers sale dates, prices, donations and reservations. A live stock list only works if someone keeps it updated, so it needs a process before it needs a page.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Open but unconvinced. "Not convinced about this - leave as a maybe."

## 5. Links to school services (breakfast club, after-school clubs, lunches)

**Feedback received:** Add relevant redirects from pages for booking breakfast and after-school clubs and lunches.

**Assessment:** Cheap and useful in principle, but the links would point at the school's own booking pages, which the school controls.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Declined. "Not doing this - these are school organised links. We are not here for that."

## 6. Per-year-group pages with logins

**Feedback received:** If the workload allows, have every year group on the site with email login access credentials, so it becomes the one place to do everything and drives engagement.

**Assessment:** Recommend against. Accounts and passwords add security and upkeep burdens to a volunteer-run static site, and parent accounts are already out of scope in the project brief.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Declined permanently. "No logins. Ever."

## 7. A downloadable app

**Feedback received:** A downloadable app as an alternative to saving the URL to the home screen for quicker access.

**Assessment:** A real app is far more than a PTA site needs. If quick access ever becomes a priority, home-screen install basics would get most of the benefit without an app.

**Nick's decision (17th October):** Declined permanently. "Again - never."
