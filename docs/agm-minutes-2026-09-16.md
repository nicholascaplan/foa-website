### 2. What is The FOA?

(HP) The Friends of Ashley is the PTA for Ashley CofE Primary School. Every staff member, parent and carer is a valued member of The FOA. The FOA helps provide a bridge between the school and the parent community by raising money to help provide the best possible future for our children and our school.

There's also a social aspect to The FOA: parents can get together to help support events, meet parents of children in different year groups and work alongside each other while raising money for the school.

At the Annual General Meeting each year, a committee of parent volunteers is elected to run The FOA for the coming academic year.

#### Who runs The FOA?

Everybody! The FOA is not just run by a small committee; it relies on an amazing team of volunteers from across the school community! A big thank you to all the helpers.

There is always a warm welcome for anyone who wants to run or support The FOA's activities. If you are interested in learning more, please contact via: [thefriendsofashley@gmail.com](mailto:thefriendsofashley@gmail.com).

### 3. The FOA committee structure

The FOA is a registered charity, and our governance follows the model constitution which can be found online at ParentKind — a resource for UK-based PTAs.

Our Constitution stipulates how your committee is elected:

> Nominations for election to roles on the committee may be made by any member of the association and seconded by another. Such nominations must have the consent of the nominee. Nominations should be made in writing to the Chair at any time until the election process has been completed. If no nominations or an insufficient number are received before the AGM, any members present may nominate a person, with their consent, and that person may be appointed by a majority vote of those present.

The structure of the committee is as follows:

- 2 Co-Chairs
- 2 Co-Secretaries
- 2 Co-Treasurers
- 2 Co-Comms
- 1 Fundraising Initiatives Lead (this is a new role introduced for 2026/2027)

### 5. Spend requests

- **A visualiser for each classroom — estimated £8,000**
- **Additional playground equipment — estimated £1,000**
- **New iPad — cost to be determined by fundraising**
- **Beekeeping suits — estimated £5,000**

Mrs Ratcliff talked through the value of each of the resources applied for through the spend requests.

#### Visualisers

Each classroom would have access to a device that works like a camera/video recorder — that is portable and can attach to the class computer. It works as both a way to enlarge and share content from the screen but also can be used to share physical objects more easily with the wider class or record best practice in handwriting so that the children have a clear model to follow. Modern equivalent of an overhead projector (OHP).

#### Playground equipment

This is a request for additional playground equipment — with 600 children in the school they do wear down the school playtime offerings quite quickly and these need to be refreshed on a regular basis.

#### iPads

Although the school is keen to reduce screen time as much as possible, the iPads are used to deliver the computer skill curriculum. At present there are 20 iPads across the entire school, but the aim would be for there to be 15 across each year group.

#### Beekeeping suits

The school would like to broaden out the beekeeping to the whole school. At present it is focused on KS1. With the purchase of additional suits in sizes suitable for both adults and older children — the KS2 pupils will also be able to include this activity alongside their curriculum.

#### Questions from the audience

**Has the school looked at places that share refurbished or second-hand iPads?** Yes, the school already works with donations of laptops from parents via their businesses and would be interested in any refurbished donations. Any contact details for businesses that routinely refurbish and share devices to schools would be very welcome.

**Do they need to be iPads?** Yes, as the existing technology is iPads the IT team who support the school recommend keeping to the same devices (for support and keeping the consistent experience for the pupils).

**Does the school access existing funding via Church status?** Yes, the school applies for all and any funding available from local and national sources as soon as it appears. The installation of the polytunnels on the school grounds (to allow whole-class year-round learning via growing/making food from the produce) is an example of receiving local council funding. Suggestion from the audience that these grants and donations be highlighted in the school newsletter to make parents aware of other ways they can support the school through their own connections. Many businesses have a charitable donation/social outreach programme.

**It is great to have these smaller items to work towards when fundraising across the school and have a tracker set up so we can celebrate them as they are ticked off** (suggestion from The FOA).

**The above spend requests were approved by those present and will be the focus of this year's fundraising across the school community.**

#### Event guidelines — a reminder

- Event leads will always be easy to identify as they must be in hi-vis for the entire event. No one else is in hi-vis to avoid confusion.
- Event leads must not consume alcohol prior to or during an event.
- The only people to whom cash may be passed are The FOA committee members or event leads.
- Bar leads are always approved by a licensed publican as per our licence.
- Please remember that the safety of the children and the community is always our first priority.

#### Questions from the audience

**Are external vendors allowed at Christmas Fayre?** Yes, external vendors are allowed at events; we charge a pitch fee — however it is down to the lead to decide if they will be part of any given event. The only challenge of external vendors is that they divert money away from our own fundraising pot.

**Will there be a quiz again?** General feeling — and the Quiz Masters were present — that this event will run again. It was very popular; however, we are limited in terms of size in relation to a suitable space. Cost and availability are challenges — this is very much a community event rather than a fundraiser. No plans to sell alcohol, as this has licensing implications, but BYO would continue.

**Why does the church charge us full price for the use of their church hall given our relationship with St Mary's?** The church are unable to give us special treatment and therefore continue to charge us the same rate as everyone else.

**What has been done with the Mini Marathon funding?** Mrs Ratcliff confirmed that the money had already been allocated to new playground and sports equipment which is being appreciated by the children. The school is already looking to sign up to both ballots for next time.

**Event feedback — would it be an idea to put up boards (as we did before for community voting) to capture a wider group for post-event feedback?** This is with The FOA committee to review and decide if they can take it up as an action. It was acknowledged that often the online surveys only seem to garner the more negative rather than constructive feedback.

**Do we need the wristbands given out to children to wear at events? These seem to cause a lot of littering.** The littering problem was acknowledged. The positioning of bins at exits, or sourcing bands that do not have small bits coming off as you use them, are being investigated. The bands are offered to parents and are optional, but a key safeguarding tool. Additionally, bands do need to be used at Fireworks to manage crowd numbers and safety.
